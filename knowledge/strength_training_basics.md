# Strength Training Basics

## Progressive Overload
Progressive overload is the most important principle in strength training. It means gradually increasing the demand placed on your muscles over time so they are forced to adapt and grow stronger. You can apply progressive overload by adding weight to the bar, doing more repetitions with the same weight, adding extra sets, slowing down the tempo of each rep, or reducing rest time between sets. A simple beginner approach is "double progression": pick a rep range such as 8 to 12 reps, and when you can complete all sets at 12 reps with good form, increase the weight by the smallest available increment and work back up from 8 reps.

## Training Splits
A training split is how you divide your workouts across the week. Common splits include:
- Full body: train every major muscle group in each session, usually 3 days per week. Great for beginners.
- Upper/Lower: alternate between upper-body and lower-body days, usually 4 days per week.
- Push/Pull/Legs (PPL): push days train chest, shoulders and triceps; pull days train back and biceps; leg days train quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves. Often run 6 days per week for intermediate lifters.
The best split is the one you can follow consistently while hitting each muscle group roughly twice per week.

## Sets, Reps and Intensity
For muscle growth (hypertrophy), most research supports roughly 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle group per week, with sets taken close to failure, typically leaving 1 to 3 reps in reserve (RIR). For pure strength, lower rep ranges of 3 to 6 reps with heavier loads and longer rest periods (2 to 5 minutes) are more effective.

## Plateaus
A plateau happens when your lifts stop improving for several weeks. Common causes are poor sleep, eating too few calories or too little protein, accumulated fatigue, and running the same program for too long. Fixes include taking a deload week (reducing volume by about 40 to 50 percent), changing exercise variations, adjusting rep ranges, and making sure protein intake is around 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram of body weight per day.

## Recovery
Muscles grow during recovery, not during the workout itself. Aim for 7 to 9 hours of sleep per night. Leave at least 48 hours before training the same muscle group hard again. Light activity such as walking can improve recovery on rest days.
